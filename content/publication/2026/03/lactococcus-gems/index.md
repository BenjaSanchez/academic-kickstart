abstract: "When developing bacterial starters for the food industry, strain diversity is essential, due to the limited genetic manipulation that can be done. Properly reflecting that diversity in computational simulations is paramount: that is why we developed a method for streamlining this process for *Lactococcus*. This included adapting an existing method (known as [carveme](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gky537)), heavily curating the knowledgebase of bacterial metabolism (known as bacterial-universe), and generating genome-scale models for all sequenced *Lactococcus* strains in NCBI. The models revealed a lot of interesting things about the diversity in the *Lactococcus* genus, including different growth behavior under a number of substrates. This project was driven by Jildau and counted with my co-supervision: It was a delight to work with her, and it was very fulfilling to see the work finally published after she successfully completed her PhD. I hope the approach is used by more in the field, and that both the curated universe and the *Lactococcus* models are useful as a knowledge resource."
